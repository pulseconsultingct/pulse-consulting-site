Problem we solve

Organize inventory and order tracking

If you can't quickly say what you have in stock, which orders are in progress, and what needs reordering, you are probably tracking it in too many places. Getting everything into one clear system is often the most useful change a small shop can make.

A short survey, no obligation. We review it before we talk.

Signs your tracking has outgrown how you do it

  • Stock counts live in a spreadsheet, a notebook, and someone's head
  • You find out an item is out of stock when a customer asks for it
  • Orders are tracked across emails, texts, and paper tickets
  • Custom or made-to-order jobs are hard to follow from start to finish
  • Only one person really knows where everything is

What a better setup includes

  • One place that holds the current list of items, quantities, and locations
  • Order records with a status, such as received, in progress, ready, or delivered
  • Alerts when an item drops below a level you choose
  • A way to look up an order or a customer quickly
  • Simple reports, such as what sold, what is slow, and what to reorder

Start with the mapping, not the software

Before choosing any tool, we map what you track today: what the items are, how they're named, who updates the records, and when. Most inventory trouble comes from inconsistent habits and messy records, not from missing software.

From there, the answer might be a well-organized shared spreadsheet, an off-the-shelf inventory or order system, or a small custom tool. We recommend the simplest option that fits, and we'll say so if a proper inventory product is better than anything we could build.

Where AI helps

AI can turn incoming emails, invoices, and order forms into organized records, so you aren't retyping them. It can also help clean up an existing list by spotting duplicates and inconsistent names. As with any data entry, we set it up so a person can review what it wrote before it becomes the official record.

Costs and limits to know about

  • Moving your existing records into a new system takes effort, and that cleanup is usually the largest part of the job.
  • Barcode scanning and label printing require hardware, which is a separate cost.
  • A system only works if people update it. We aim to make the update step quick, because a tool that is annoying to use gets skipped.
  • Businesses with very large or complicated inventories may be better served by dedicated inventory software.

Common questions

Do I need special software to track inventory?

Not always. Many small shops do well with an organized shared spreadsheet. We help you decide when it's time to move to something more capable.

Can this handle custom or made-to-order work?

Yes. Order tracking with clear statuses is well suited to custom jobs, where each order has its own details and deadline.

How long does it take to get organized?

It depends on how much data you have and how consistent it is. We'll give you a realistic picture after reviewing what you track today.

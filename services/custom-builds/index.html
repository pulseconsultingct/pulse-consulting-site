Custom AI tools and integrations for small businesses

Sometimes the software you can buy doesn't match how your business works, and no amount of settings will change that. A small custom build can close the gap, as long as it is kept simple enough to maintain.

When custom makes sense

Custom work is worth considering when two systems you depend on don't connect, when your process is unusual and every product forces you to change it, or when staff spend real time on a task no existing tool handles.

It is usually not worth it when an existing product does 90 percent of the job. In that case we will tell you to buy the product.

Examples of what we build

  • An integration that moves information between two systems that don't talk to each other
  • A simple intake form that feeds your records and notifies the right person
  • An internal assistant that answers staff questions using your own documents and procedures
  • A small dashboard that pulls numbers from several places into one view
  • A tool that reads incoming emails or files and organizes the information for review

How a build goes

  1. Scope: we agree in writing on one clear problem, what done looks like, and what is left out.
  2. Build small: a working first version you can try, before anything is polished.
  3. Test with your real data and your real staff, then fix what doesn't hold up.
  4. Hand off: documentation, plus a plain explanation of how to keep it running.

The honest part about custom software

Anything custom needs upkeep. The services it connects to change, accounts expire, and your business changes too. We design for the simplest approach that works, because simple things are cheaper to maintain and easier to hand to someone else.

Before building, we agree in writing on who owns the work, who maintains it, and what happens if something breaks. That conversation is part of the project, not an afterthought.

What we won't build

We stay away from tools where a wrong answer could cause serious harm and no person reviews the output. We also won't build something we can't explain to you in plain terms.

Common questions

How is this different from buying software?

Software you buy is built for many businesses. A custom build is made for yours, which can fit better but comes with more responsibility for upkeep. We help you decide which makes sense.

Can I see something working before I commit to the full build?

That is the point of building a small first version. You can try it with real examples before deciding whether to expand it.

Who owns what you build?

We settle that in writing before the work starts, along with who is responsible for maintaining it.
